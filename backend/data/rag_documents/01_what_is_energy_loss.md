# Energy Loss in a Private Generator Network

Energy loss is the difference between what the generators produce and what
customers are billed for.

    loss = produced_kwh - billed_kwh
    loss_pct = loss / produced_kwh * 100

Loss is NOT one thing. It has five distinct causes, and confusing them is
the most common analytical mistake.

## 1. Normal technical loss
Electricity turning into heat inside the cables. Governed by I squared
times R: doubling the current makes the loss four times larger. Unavoidable.
A private generator network suffers more of this than a utility network
because it distributes at 220/380 V with no step-down transformer, so the
currents are far higher for the same power.
Typical range: 4 to 8 percent.

## 2. Excess technical loss
The same physics, made worse by poor construction: taped joints instead of
crimped ones, undersized aluminium cable, corroded outdoor connections,
unbalanced phases, low power factor. This is FIXABLE and is often the
cheapest saving available to the owner.
Typical range: 0 to 6 percent.

## 3. Measurement and process loss
The energy was delivered and used correctly but recorded wrongly: slow
mechanical meters, OCR misreads, estimated readings, meters read on
different days of the month, meter replacements that reset to zero.
Typical range: 1 to 3 percent.

## 4. Unbilled connections
Supply the owner gives away or uses himself: the control room, the fuel
pump, his own house, a mosque, employees on free supply. Not theft, but it
appears as missing energy unless it is recorded.

## 5. Non-technical loss (theft)
Electricity taken without being recorded: direct hooks before the meter,
partial bypass of a house circuit, magnets or jammed meters, reversed
meters, self-reconnection after disconnection.
Typical range: 2 to 10 percent.

## The rule
Theft is what remains AFTER the other four have been accounted for. Never
report the raw gap as theft.
