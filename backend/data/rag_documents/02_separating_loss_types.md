# How to Tell Technical Loss From Theft

Four diagnostic tests, all possible with generator data and monthly meter
readings. No extra hardware is required.

## Test 1 - load dependence
Technical loss follows current squared, so it rises and falls with load.
Theft does not care about load.
  loss % rises in summer and falls in spring  -> technical
  loss % flat all year regardless of load     -> a fixed draw, likely a hook

## Test 2 - concentration
  loss spread thinly across every customer on the feeder -> cable
  loss traceable to two or three specific meters         -> those meters

## Test 3 - shape over time
  slow upward drift across years -> ageing cable, corrosion
  sudden step in one month, then flat -> theft began that month

## Test 4 - zone comparison (the most powerful)
Zones on the same generator experience the same weather, tariff and fuel.
If one zone loses 12 percent while another loses 5 percent at similar load,
the difference is a property of that zone: its cable, its joints, or someone
on it.

## Phase imbalance
When the three phases carry unequal current, the difference returns through
the neutral conductor, which also has resistance. That is heat serving no
customer. Imbalance above roughly 15 percent is worth investigating.

## Power factor
Motors and older air conditioners draw more current than the useful power
they consume. A power factor of 0.85 instead of 0.95 means about 25 percent
more current, and because loss follows the square, roughly 55 percent more
cable loss.
